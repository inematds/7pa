The shift

AI stopped being a tool. It became part of the team.

Before, you used AI to answer questions. Now it can interpret what you want, look up information, execute tasks and deliver a result, with more or less freedom. That changes your role.

BEFORE
You→run the process→result
NOW
You→define the intent→organize the context→delegate→supervise→evaluate

🎯 You say what, not how

Instead of configuring every step, you define the goal, what counts as a good result, the limits and when it's done. AI decides part of the path.

🗄️ Data becomes the foundation

The agent is only as good as the information it uses. Someone has to say which source is official and who wins when two sources disagree.

👔 You become the manager

You move from "doing the task" to "managing the system that does the task": vision, context, judgment, evaluation and correction.

The 7 principles

A cycle, not a list.

Each principle answers a question you'd ask a new employee. The Agent Sheet asks exactly these questions for you.

1 Intent→2 Context→3 Data→4 Criteria→5 Autonomy→6 Observation→7 Supervision↺
1

Intent

The agent needs to know the goal, why it matters and what result is expected. Without that, it does the wrong task efficiently.

The questionWhat do you want off your plate, and why?
Before delegating, define the purpose.
2

Context

Rules, examples, history, current state. But more context isn't better: the right amount is the minimum that produces the best decision.

The questionWhat would you explain on day 1 to someone newly hired?
The smallest context capable of the best decision.
3

Reliable data

Official source, consistency, freshness, traceability. Wrong data in, wrong decision out.

The questionWhere does the correct information come from? Who wins when two sources disagree?
No agent is better than its data.
4

Success criteria

Metric, expected quality, acceptance condition, definition of "done". Without it, it finishes without solving anything.

The questionWhat does a perfect day look like for this?
Every delegation needs success defined.
5

Autonomy with limits

Not every agent deserves the same freedom. It grows in levels, from N0 to N4, together with trust.

The questionWhat can it never do alone? What's the worst that can happen?
Autonomy grows with evidence and control.
6

Observation and evaluation

Record results, errors, costs, time and decisions. Execution generates evidence; evidence generates learning.

The questionWhat do you want to see to know it's going well?
What isn't observed doesn't improve.
7

Human supervision

AI executes within limits, but responsibility stays yours: goals, exceptions, corrections, evolution.

The questionWhen does it call you? When do you review?
Agents amplify; they don't replace judgment.
↺

And back to 1

What you learn in supervision adjusts intent, context and rules. That's how isolated agents become a working system.

Principle 5 in detail

The autonomy ladder

The more freedom, the more control. The agent starts low and climbs when it shows, with evidence, that it deserves to.

N0
ConsultsAnswers and researches. Doesn't act on anything.
N1
RecommendsPrepares drafts and suggestions. You decide what to use.
N2
Prepares, you approveGets everything ready, but nothing goes out without your OK. The right level whenever money, an irreversible action, or outside people are involved.
N3
ExecutesDoes it alone, within the rules, and reports back. Only for internal, reversible tasks.
N4
Manages processesRuns a whole process, with reports and alarms. Requires the strongest supervision.
# The rule the Agent Sheet uses to calculate your level
Involves money, an irreversible action, or outside people?  → at most N2 (you approve)
Internal and reversible?                                    → can reach N3
Only looks up information or drafts?                       → N0 / N1
No official source defined?                                 → stays at N1, no exceptions
What actually matters

Strip away the marketing, and this is what's left.

Reports about "the new era of agents" come loaded with promotional layers. This isn't an exclusive discovery of any one company: it's the consolidation of what already works in practice. After the filter, 5 truths remain.

1 · Reliable data

The agent finds conflicts and duplicates, but the organization decides which piece of information has authority.

2 · Automation by intent

You provide goal, context, constraints, criteria and expected result. The system chooses part of the path.

3 · Explicit success

It's not enough to say what to do. You need to say what's good, how to measure it and when it's actually done.

4 · Limits

Permissions, autonomy levels, human review, monitoring, policies and security. More autonomy, more control.

5 · The bottleneck shifted

The hard part is no longer executing. It's defining intent well, building context, organizing data, evaluating and correcting.

+ Demo isn't production

Working in a demo proves nothing. In real use, what matters is stability, repeatability, safety and how it handles the unexpected.

The new discipline isn't "using AI."
It's AI Agent Management.

User guide · Agent Sheet

Apply the 7 principles in 5 minutes

The Agent Sheet is a free page that runs in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. You answer, it builds.

1

Open the Sheet and pick a starting point

Click a ready example (clinic, store, accounting office, teacher) and adjust it to your case, or start blank.

Open the Agent Sheet
2

Answer the 7 questions, one screen at a time

Write the way you'd talk to a person. Every field has an example. On question 5, check whether the task involves money, an irreversible action, or outside people.

3

Get the ready-made package

There are 4 tabs: the Sheet (a summary of your agent's 7 principles), the Ready instruction (to paste into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini), the 3 tests and the supervision Checklist. At the top, the calculated autonomy level and what's needed to level up.

# excerpt from a generated instruction
## Your autonomy level: N2 (Prepares, you approve)
You prepare everything, but NOTHING is sent, changed or executed
without my explicit approval. Always show me first and wait for my "OK".
4

Test before you trust

Run the 3 tests: missing information, forbidden request and alarm situation. Did it fail one? Adjust the sheet. Want to polish it? The ✨ Refine with AI button copies a request for ChatGPT/Claude to review your sheet.

5

Supervise with the checklist

2 minutes a day reading the report, 10 minutes a week adjusting one rule. Autonomy only grows with evidence. When in doubt, drop a level.

Real example

Dr. Ana and the Appointment Confirmer

30% of patients missed appointments without notice. In 5 minutes with the Sheet, she came out with this:

🎯 Mission

Confirm next day's appointments via WhatsApp, to reduce no-shows.

🗄️ Source of truth

The clinic's scheduling system. Spreadsheets don't count. Something odd: it flags it, never guesses.

🛡️ Level N2: prepares, she approves

Because it talks to patients and a sent message can't be undone. Forbidden: rescheduling, cancelling, discussing prices. Moves to N3 after 4 error-free weeks.

👁️ Report and review

At 5pm: sent, confirmed, no response, errors. Calls right away if there's a complaint. Review every Friday, 10 minutes.
